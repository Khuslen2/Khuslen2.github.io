# Irodov 1.1 — Motorboat and Raft

[← Back to Irodov problems](/)

## Problem

A motorboat travelling downstream passes a raft at point \(A\).  
After travelling for \(60\) minutes, the motorboat turns back and later meets the raft again \(6.0\,\mathrm{km}\) downstream from point \(A\).

Find the velocity of the river flow.

---

## Given

\[
\tau = 60\,\mathrm{min}
\]

\[
l = 6.0\,\mathrm{km}
\]

Find:

\[
v_f = ?
\]

where \(v_f\) is the velocity of the river flow.

---

## My First Approach

I first tried solving the problem from the reference frame of the river bank.

Let

- \(v_f\) = velocity of the river flow
- \(v_m\) = velocity of the motorboat relative to the water

Since the raft simply moves with the river,

\[
v_{\mathrm{raft}} = v_f.
\]

When the motorboat travels downstream, its velocity relative to the bank is

\[
v_{\mathrm{down}} = v_m + v_f.
\]

After the motorboat turns around, its upstream velocity has magnitude

\[
v_{\mathrm{up}} = v_m - v_f.
\]

This approach can be used to construct equations for the positions of the boat and raft.

However, there is a much simpler way to look at the problem.

---

## A Simpler Approach — Reference Frame of the Raft

Instead of observing the motion from the river bank, consider the motion **relative to the raft**.

The raft moves together with the water. Therefore, in the raft's reference frame, the water is stationary.

The motorboat moves away from the raft with speed \(v_m\).

It travels downstream for

\[
\tau = 60\,\mathrm{min}.
\]

Therefore, the distance between the motorboat and the raft when the motorboat turns around is

\[
d = v_m\tau.
\]

After turning around, the motorboat approaches the raft with the same speed \(v_m\) relative to the water.

Therefore, the time required to return to the raft is also

\[
\tau = 60\,\mathrm{min}.
\]

So the total time during which the raft drifts downstream is

\[
T = 2\tau.
\]

Thus,

\[
T = 2(60\,\mathrm{min})
   = 120\,\mathrm{min}
   = 2\,\mathrm{h}.
\]

During these two hours, the raft travels

\[
l = 6.0\,\mathrm{km}.
\]

Since the raft moves with the river,

\[
l = v_f T.
\]

Therefore,

\[
v_f = \frac{l}{T}.
\]

Substituting the values,

\[
v_f
=
\frac{6.0\,\mathrm{km}}{2\,\mathrm{h}}
=
3.0\,\mathrm{km/h}.
\]

Converting to SI units,

\[
v_f
=
\frac{6.0\times10^3}
{2\times60\times60}
\approx
0.83\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\]

---

## Final Answer

\[
\boxed{v_f \approx 0.83\,\mathrm{m/s}}
\]

---

## What I Learned

The main idea in this problem is not complicated algebra, but the **choice of reference frame**.

From the river bank, the motorboat velocities involve both \(v_m\) and \(v_f\), which makes the equations more complicated.

From the raft's reference frame, the river flow disappears from the relative motion. The motorboat travels away for one hour and takes one hour to return.

Therefore, the raft has been drifting for two hours.

This problem is a good example of how choosing a convenient reference frame can greatly simplify a mechanics problem.
