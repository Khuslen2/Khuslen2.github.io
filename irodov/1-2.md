# Irodov 1.2 — Mean Velocity

[← Back to Irodov problems](/)

## Problem

A point moves along a straight line.

It covers the first half of the total distance with velocity $v_0$.

For the remaining half of the distance, it moves with velocity $v_1$ for half of the time and with velocity $v_2$ for the other half.

Find the mean velocity over the entire motion.

---

## Given

Let the total distance be

$$
x.
$$

For the first half of the distance,

$$
\text{distance} = \frac{x}{2},
\qquad
\text{velocity} = v_0.
$$

For the second half, the point moves for equal time intervals with velocities $v_1$ and $v_2$.

Find:

$$
\langle v \rangle = ?
$$

---

## My Approach

The mean velocity over the whole motion is

$$
\langle v \rangle
=
\frac{\text{total distance}}
{\text{total time}}.
$$

Therefore, instead of directly averaging $v_0$, $v_1$, and $v_2$, I first need to find the total time of motion.

Let

- $t_0$ = time required to cover the first half of the distance,
- $t'$ = each of the two equal time intervals during the second half.

The total time is therefore

$$
T = t_0 + 2t'.
$$

Hence,

$$
\langle v \rangle
=
\frac{x}{t_0+2t'}.
$$

---

## First Half of the Distance

The first half of the total distance is

$$
\frac{x}{2}.
$$

Since this part is covered with velocity $v_0$,

$$
\frac{x}{2}=v_0t_0.
$$

Therefore,

$$
t_0=\frac{x}{2v_0}.
$$

---

## Second Half of the Distance

The remaining distance is also

$$
\frac{x}{2}.
$$

During this part, the point moves with velocity $v_1$ for time $t'$ and with velocity $v_2$ for another equal time $t'$.

The distances travelled during these intervals are

$$
x_1=v_1t'
$$

and

$$
x_2=v_2t'.
$$

Together they make up the second half of the total distance:

$$
\frac{x}{2}=v_1t'+v_2t'.
$$

Factoring out $t'$,

$$
\frac{x}{2}=(v_1+v_2)t'.
$$

Thus,

$$
t'=\frac{x}{2(v_1+v_2)}.
$$

Since there are two equal time intervals, the total time for the second half is

$$
2t'
=
\frac{x}{v_1+v_2}.
$$

---

## Mean Velocity

The total time of motion is

$$
T
=
t_0+2t'.
$$

Substituting the expressions found above,

$$
T
=
\frac{x}{2v_0}
+
\frac{x}{v_1+v_2}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\langle v\rangle
=
\frac{x}
{\frac{x}{2v_0}+\frac{x}{v_1+v_2}}.
$$

Canceling $x$,

$$
\langle v\rangle
=
\frac{1}
{\frac{1}{2v_0}+\frac{1}{v_1+v_2}}.
$$

Combining the fractions,

$$
\langle v\rangle
=
\frac{2v_0(v_1+v_2)}
{2v_0+v_1+v_2}.
$$

---

## Final Answer

$$
\boxed{
\langle v\rangle
=
\frac{2v_0(v_1+v_2)}
{2v_0+v_1+v_2}
}
$$

---

## What I Learned

The important point in this problem is that **mean velocity is not generally the arithmetic mean of the given velocities**.

It must be calculated from

$$
\langle v\rangle
=
\frac{\text{total displacement}}
{\text{total time}}.
$$

The first half of the journey is specified by **distance**, while the second half is divided according to **time**.

In particular, during the second half, $v_1$ and $v_2$ act for equal times, but they do not cover equal distances.

Therefore,

$$
v_1t' \neq v_2t'
$$

in general.

Recognizing whether a problem divides the motion by **distance** or by **time** is essential when calculating average velocity.
