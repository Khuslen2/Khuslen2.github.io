# Irodov Problem 2.27 — Sudden Stoppage of a Gas-Filled Vessel

[← Back to 2.2 — The First Law of Thermodynamics. Heat Capacity](/irodov/part-2/first-law.html)

## Problem

A thermally insulated vessel contains an ideal gas of molar mass $$M$$. The ratio of its specific heats is

$$
\gamma=\frac{C_p}{C_v}.
$$

The vessel and the gas inside it initially move together with velocity $$v$$. The vessel is then stopped suddenly.

Find the resulting increase in the temperature of the gas.

---

## Physical Idea

Before the vessel stops, the gas moves together with the vessel and therefore has **bulk kinetic energy**.

If the total mass of the gas is $$m$$, this kinetic energy is

$$
K=\frac{1}{2}mv^2.
$$

When the vessel is suddenly stopped, the gas initially continues moving because of its inertia. Through collisions and internal processes, this organized bulk motion eventually disappears.

Since the vessel is thermally insulated, no heat is transferred through its walls:

$$
Q=0.
$$

The lost bulk kinetic energy is converted into internal energy of the gas:

$$
\Delta U=K.
$$

Therefore, the gas temperature increases.

---

## My Solution

For an ideal gas, the change in internal energy is

$$
\Delta U=nC_v\Delta T.
$$

The specific-heat ratio is

$$
\gamma=\frac{C_p}{C_v},
$$

and for an ideal gas,

$$
C_p-C_v=R.
$$

From

$$
C_p=\gamma C_v,
$$

we obtain

$$
\gamma C_v-C_v=R.
$$

Therefore,

$$
C_v(\gamma-1)=R,
$$

so

$$
C_v=\frac{R}{\gamma-1}.
$$

Substituting this into the internal-energy equation,

$$
\Delta U
=
n\frac{R}{\gamma-1}\Delta T.
$$

The initial bulk kinetic energy of the gas is

$$
K=\frac{1}{2}mv^2.
$$

By energy conservation,

$$
n\frac{R}{\gamma-1}\Delta T
=
\frac{1}{2}mv^2.
$$

The molar mass is $$M$$, so the total gas mass is

$$
m=nM.
$$

Therefore,

$$
n\frac{R}{\gamma-1}\Delta T
=
\frac{1}{2}nMv^2.
$$

Canceling $$n$$ gives

$$
\frac{R}{\gamma-1}\Delta T
=
\frac{1}{2}Mv^2.
$$

Thus,

$$
\Delta T
=
\frac{(\gamma-1)Mv^2}{2R}.
$$

---

## Final Answer

$$
\boxed{
\Delta T=
\frac{(\gamma-1)Mv^2}{2R}
}
$$

---

## My Mistake

In my first solution, I wrote

$$
Q=\Delta U=nC_v\Delta T.
$$

This is not correct for this process.

Because the vessel is **thermally insulated**, there is no heat transfer between the gas and its surroundings:

$$
Q=0.
$$

However, this does not mean that the internal energy cannot increase.

The increase in internal energy comes from the conversion of the gas's initial bulk kinetic energy:

$$
K_{\text{bulk}}
\rightarrow
U.
$$

Therefore, the correct energy relation is

$$
\Delta U=\frac{1}{2}mv^2.
$$

---

## What I Learned

I learned that an increase in internal energy does **not necessarily mean that heat entered the system**.

Heat $$Q$$ is energy transferred because of a temperature difference. In this problem, the vessel is thermally insulated, so

$$
Q=0.
$$

Nevertheless, the gas can gain internal energy because its organized macroscopic motion is converted into random molecular motion when the vessel suddenly stops.

I also learned how the given ratio

$$
\gamma=\frac{C_p}{C_v}
$$

can be combined with

$$
C_p-C_v=R
$$

to obtain

$$
C_v=\frac{R}{\gamma-1}.
$$

This is why the problem gives $$\gamma$$ even though the final calculation of the temperature increase uses $$C_v$$.
