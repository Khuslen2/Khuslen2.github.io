# Irodov Problem 2.2 — Two Vessels Connected by a Valve

[← Back to 2.1 — Equation of the Gas State. Processes](/irodov/part-2/gas-state.html)

## Problem

Two identical rigid vessels are connected by a valve. The valve allows gas to pass from one vessel to the other whenever the pressure difference reaches $$1.10\,\text{atm}$$.

Initially, one vessel contains an ideal gas at $$27^\circ\text{C}$$ and $$1.00\,\text{atm}$$, while the other vessel is evacuated. Both vessels are then heated to $$107^\circ\text{C}$$.

Find the final pressure in the vessel that was initially evacuated.

---

## Physical Idea

At first, the second vessel is a vacuum, so

$$
p_2=0.
$$

As the vessels are heated, the gas initially remains entirely in the first vessel. Since its volume and amount of gas are constant during this stage,

$$
\frac{p}{T}=\text{constant}.
$$

The valve begins to let gas through only when the pressure difference reaches

$$
\Delta p=1.10\,\text{atm}.
$$

However, finding when the valve first opens is only the **first stage** of the problem.

After the valve opens, gas enters the second vessel while heating continues. At the final temperature, the total amount of gas is conserved and the pressure difference between the two vessels remains at the valve threshold.

---

## My Solution

The initial temperature is

$$
T_1=27+273=300\,\text{K}.
$$

The final temperature is

$$
T_2=107+273=380\,\text{K}.
$$

Initially,

$$
p_1=1.00\,\text{atm}.
$$

### When does the valve open?

Before the valve opens, the amount of gas and the volume of the first vessel remain constant. Therefore,

$$
\frac{p}{T}=\frac{p_1}{T_1}.
$$

Because the second vessel is initially a vacuum, the valve begins to open when the pressure in the first vessel reaches

$$
p=1.10\,\text{atm}.
$$

Thus,

$$
\frac{1.10}{T'}
=
\frac{1.00}{300}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
T'=330\,\text{K}.
$$

Converting back to Celsius,

$$
t'=330-273=57^\circ\text{C}.
$$

So the valve begins to open at approximately

$$
57^\circ\text{C}.
$$

### What happens after the valve opens?

Heating continues until

$$
T_2=380\,\text{K}.
$$

Let $$p$$ be the final pressure in the vessel that was initially evacuated.

Because the valve maintains a pressure difference of $$1.10\,\text{atm}$$, the final pressure in the original gas vessel is

$$
p+1.10.
$$

Let the volume of each vessel be $$V$$.

The initial total amount of gas is

$$
n
=
\frac{p_1V}{RT_1}.
$$

At the final temperature, the same total amount of gas is distributed between the two vessels:

$$
n
=
\frac{pV}{RT_2}
+
\frac{(p+1.10)V}{RT_2}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\frac{p_1V}{RT_1}
=
\frac{pV+(p+1.10)V}{RT_2}.
$$

Canceling $$V$$ and $$R$$,

$$
\frac{p_1}{T_1}
=
\frac{2p+1.10}{T_2}.
$$

Substituting the known values,

$$
\frac{1.00}{300}
=
\frac{2p+1.10}{380}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
2p+1.10
=
\frac{380}{300}.
$$

Hence,

$$
2p+1.10=1.2667,
$$

so

$$
2p=0.1667.
$$

Therefore,

$$
p=0.0833\,\text{atm}.
$$

---

## Final Answer

The pressure in the vessel that was initially evacuated becomes

$$
\boxed{p\approx8.3\times10^{-2}\,\text{atm}}
$$

---

## My Mistake

In my first attempt, I correctly found the temperature at which the valve begins to open:

$$
t'\approx57^\circ\text{C}.
$$

However, I treated this as if it solved the whole problem.

The vessels are actually heated further, from about $$57^\circ\text{C}$$ to $$107^\circ\text{C}$$. Once the valve opens, gas begins entering the vessel that was initially evacuated.

Therefore, the problem has **two stages**:

1. Before the valve opens, all the gas remains in one vessel.
2. After the valve opens, the gas is distributed between both vessels while the pressure difference is maintained at $$1.10\,\text{atm}$$.

The second stage requires conservation of the total amount of gas.

---

## What I Learned

My calculation of approximately $$57^\circ\text{C}$$ was not wrong. It answered a different question: **when does the valve begin to open?**

The actual problem asks for the pressure after heating continues to $$107^\circ\text{C}$$.

I learned that in problems involving valves or other threshold conditions, reaching the threshold can mark the beginning of a new physical stage rather than the end of the process.

I also learned that a vacuum has zero gas pressure in the idealized model, but once the valve opens, gas enters the evacuated vessel and its pressure becomes nonzero.
