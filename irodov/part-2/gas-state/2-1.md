# Irodov Problem 2.1 — Gas Released from a Vessel

## Problem

A rigid vessel of volume $$30\,\text{L}$$ contains an ideal gas at $$0^\circ\text{C}$$. Some of the gas is released while the temperature remains constant, causing the pressure inside the vessel to decrease by $$0.78\,\text{atm}$$.

The density of the gas under normal conditions is $$1.3\,\text{g/L}$$. Find the mass of gas that escaped.

---

## Physical Idea

At first, I thought I needed to know the initial pressure or the initial number of moles. However, the important information is the **change in pressure**.

The vessel is rigid, so its volume does not change:

$$
V=30\,\text{L}.
$$

The temperature also remains constant.

For an ideal gas,

$$
pV=nRT.
$$

Before the gas is released,

$$
pV=nRT.
$$

After some gas escapes,

$$
p'V=n'RT.
$$

Subtracting the second equation from the first gives

$$
(p-p')V=(n-n')RT.
$$

Therefore, the pressure decrease corresponds directly to the amount of gas that escaped.

---

## My Solution

The pressure decrease is

$$
\Delta p=p-p'=0.78\,\text{atm}.
$$

Since the temperature is $$0^\circ\text{C}$$, the same as the temperature used for the given normal-condition density, I can determine what volume the escaped gas would occupy at normal pressure.

Let this volume be $$V_{\text{released}}$$.

At normal conditions,

$$
p_0=1\,\text{atm}.
$$

For the amount of gas that escaped,

$$
p_0V_{\text{released}}=\Delta p\,V.
$$

Therefore,

$$
V_{\text{released}}
=
\frac{\Delta p\,V}{p_0}.
$$

Substituting the values,

$$
V_{\text{released}}
=
\frac{(0.78\,\text{atm})(30\,\text{L})}
{1\,\text{atm}}.
$$

The pressure units cancel, leaving a volume:

$$
V_{\text{released}}=23.4\,\text{L}.
$$

The density of the gas under normal conditions is

$$
\rho=1.3\,\text{g/L}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
m=\rho V_{\text{released}}.
$$

Thus,

$$
m
=
(1.3\,\text{g/L})(23.4\,\text{L})
=
30.42\,\text{g}.
$$

---

## Final Answer

$$
\boxed{m\approx30.4\,\text{g}}
$$

---

## My Mistake

In my first attempt, I used

$$
pV=p'V'.
$$

This was not valid for this situation.

That relation would compare two states of the **same fixed amount of gas**. In this problem, however, some gas leaves the vessel, so the number of moles changes.

Also, the vessel itself is rigid, so its volume does not change:

$$
V'=V.
$$

The quantities that actually change are the pressure and the amount of gas:

$$
p\rightarrow p',
$$

$$
n\rightarrow n'.
$$

Therefore, the correct equations for the two states are

$$
pV=nRT
$$

and

$$
p'V=n'RT.
$$

Subtracting them gives

$$
(p-p')V=(n-n')RT.
$$

This shows why the given pressure decrease is enough to determine how much gas escaped.

---

## What I Learned

I originally thought that because the initial number of moles was unknown, I could not determine the initial pressure and therefore could not solve the problem.

But the initial pressure is not actually needed. Since the **volume and temperature remain constant**, the pressure difference tells us how much gas has left the vessel.

I also learned to pay more attention to units. Multiplying

$$
(0.78\,\text{atm})(30\,\text{L})
$$

does **not** directly give $$23.4\,\text{L}$$.

The unit of that product is

$$
\text{atm}\cdot\text{L}.
$$

To obtain the volume that the escaped gas would occupy under normal conditions, I must divide by the normal pressure:

$$
V_{\text{released}}
=
\frac{(0.78\,\text{atm})(30\,\text{L})}
{1\,\text{atm}}.
$$

Now the pressure units cancel:

$$
\frac{\text{atm}\cdot\text{L}}{\text{atm}}
=
\text{L}.
$$

This problem showed me that checking units is not only a way to check a calculation; it can also help reveal what an equation physically means.
