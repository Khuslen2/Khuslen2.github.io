# Irodov Problem 2.1 — Gas Released from a Vessel

## Problem

A rigid vessel of volume \(30\,\text{L}\) contains an ideal gas at \(0^\circ\text{C}\). Some of the gas is released while the temperature remains constant, causing the pressure inside the vessel to decrease by \(0.78\,\text{atm}\).

The density of the gas under normal conditions is \(1.3\,\text{g/L}\). Find the mass of gas that escaped.

---

## Physical Idea

At first, I thought I needed to know the initial pressure or the initial number of moles. However, the important information is the **change in pressure**.

The vessel is rigid, so its volume does not change:

$$
V = 30\,\text{L}.
$$

The temperature also remains constant.

For an ideal gas,

$$
pV=nRT.
$$

Before the gas is released,

$$
pV=nRT.
$$

After some gas escapes,

$$
p'V=n'RT.
$$

Subtracting the second equation from the first gives

$$
(p-p')V=(n-n')RT.
$$

Therefore, the pressure decrease corresponds directly to the amount of gas that escaped.

---

## My Solution

The pressure decrease is

$$
\Delta p=p-p'=0.78\,\text{atm}.
$$

Since the temperature is \(0^\circ\text{C}\), the same as the temperature used for the given normal-condition density, I can determine what volume the escaped gas would occupy at normal pressure.

Let this volume be \(V_{\text{released}}\).

At normal conditions,

$$
p_0=1\,\text{atm}.
$$

For the amount of gas that escaped,

$$
p_0V_{\text{released}}=\Delta p\,V.
$$

Therefore,

$$
V_{\text{released}}
=
\frac{\Delta p\,V}{p_0}.
$$

Substituting the values,

$$
V_{\text{released}}
=
\frac{(0.78\,\text{atm})(30\,\text{L})}
{1\,\text{atm}}.
$$

The pressure units cancel:

$$
V_{\text{released}}=23.4\,\text{L}.
$$

The density of the gas under these conditions is

$$
\rho=1.3\,\text{g/L}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
m=\rho V_{\text{released}},
$$

so

$$
m
=
(1.3\,\text{g/L})(23.4\,\text{L})
=
30.42\,\text{g}.
$$

---

## Final Answer

$$
\boxed{m\approx30.4\,\text{g}}
$$

---

## My Mistake

My first attempt used

$$
pV=p'V'.
$$

This was not valid for this situation.

That relation would compare two states of the **same fixed amount of gas**. Here, however, some gas leaves the vessel, so the number of moles changes.

Also, the vessel itself is rigid. Its volume does not become a new value \(V'\):

$$
V'=V.
$$

The quantities that change are instead

$$
p\rightarrow p'
$$

and

$$
n\rightarrow n'.
$$

So the correct way to compare the states is

$$
pV=nRT,
$$

$$
p'V=n'RT,
$$

and then subtract the two equations.

---

## What I Learned

I originally thought that because the initial number of moles was unknown, I could not determine the initial pressure and therefore could not solve the problem.

But the initial pressure is not actually needed. The **pressure difference** tells us how much gas has left when the vessel volume and temperature remain constant.

I also learned to be careful with units. Multiplying

$$
(0.78\,\text{atm})(30\,\text{L})
$$

does **not** directly give \(23.4\,\text{L}\). Its unit is

$$
\text{atm}\cdot\text{L}.
$$

The volume appears only after dividing by the normal pressure:

$$
V_{\text{released}}
=
\frac{(0.78\,\text{atm})(30\,\text{L})}
{1\,\text{atm}},
$$

so the \(\text{atm}\) units cancel and the result is correctly expressed in liters.

This problem helped me see why checking units is useful for understanding the physics, not just for checking the final calculation.
