# Irodov Problem 2.63 — Pressure of Partially Dissociated Nitrogen

[← Back to 2.3 — Kinetic Theory of Gases](/irodov/part-2/kinetic-theory.html)

## Problem

A vessel of volume $$5.0\,\text{L}$$ contains $$1.4\,\text{g}$$ of nitrogen at a temperature of $$1800\,\text{K}$$.

At this temperature, $$30\%$$ of the nitrogen molecules dissociate into atoms.

Find the pressure of the gas.

---

## Physical Idea

Initially, the nitrogen exists as molecular nitrogen:

$$
N_2.
$$

At the high temperature, some of these molecules dissociate according to

$$
N_2\rightarrow2N.
$$

Therefore, every mole of $$N_2$$ that dissociates produces **two moles of nitrogen atoms**.

The important point is that $$30\%$$ dissociation means that $$30\%$$ of the original $$N_2$$ molecules dissociate. It does not simply mean that $$30\%$$ of the total mass should be treated as atomic nitrogen.

Because dissociation increases the total number of gas particles, it also increases the pressure.

---

## My Solution

The molar mass of molecular nitrogen is

$$
M_{N_2}=28\,\text{g/mol}.
$$

Therefore, the initial amount of nitrogen is

$$
n_0=\frac{m}{M_{N_2}}.
$$

Substituting the values,

$$
n_0
=
\frac{1.4}{28}
=
0.050\,\text{mol}.
$$

Since $$30\%$$ of the original molecules dissociate, $$70\%$$ remain as $$N_2$$.

Therefore,

$$
n_{N_2}
=
0.70n_0
=
0.70(0.050)
=
0.035\,\text{mol}.
$$

The amount of $$N_2$$ that dissociates is

$$
n_{\text{diss}}
=
0.30n_0
=
0.015\,\text{mol}.
$$

Since

$$
N_2\rightarrow2N,
$$

each mole of dissociated $$N_2$$ produces two moles of nitrogen atoms.

Thus,

$$
n_N
=
2n_{\text{diss}}
=
2(0.015)
=
0.030\,\text{mol}.
$$

The total amount of gas particles is therefore

$$
n
=
n_{N_2}+n_N.
$$

Hence,

$$
n
=
0.035+0.030
=
0.065\,\text{mol}.
$$

Now I use the ideal gas law,

$$
pV=nRT.
$$

Therefore,

$$
p=\frac{nRT}{V}.
$$

The volume is

$$
V=5.0\,\text{L}
=
5.0\times10^{-3}\,\text{m}^3.
$$

Substituting,

$$
p
=
\frac{
(0.065)(8.314)(1800)
}{
5.0\times10^{-3}
}.
$$

This gives

$$
p\approx1.95\times10^5\,\text{Pa}.
$$

---

## Final Answer

$$
\boxed{p\approx1.9\times10^5\,\text{Pa}}
$$

or approximately

$$
\boxed{p\approx1.9\,\text{atm}}.
$$

---

## My Mistake

In my first solution, I interpreted the $$30\%$$ dissociation as if the original $$1.4\,\text{g}$$ of nitrogen could simply be divided into

$$
70\%
$$

molecular nitrogen and

$$
30\%
$$

atomic nitrogen by mass.

This is not the correct meaning of the dissociation percentage.

The $$30\%$$ refers to the fraction of the **original $$N_2$$ molecules that dissociate**.

The correct process is

$$
N_2\rightarrow2N.
$$

Therefore, I should first calculate the original amount of $$N_2$$ and then apply the dissociation fraction.

Interestingly, my original method still gave

$$
n=0.065\,\text{mol},
$$

so my final pressure happened to be correct. However, the physical interpretation behind that calculation was not correct.

---

## What I Learned

For a dissociation problem, I should think about the **chemical reaction and the number of particles**, rather than simply dividing the mass according to the given percentage.

Starting with

$$
n_0=0.050\,\text{mol}
$$

of $$N_2$$, the dissociation

$$
N_2\rightarrow2N
$$

means that each dissociated molecule becomes two particles.

Therefore, dissociation increases the total number of gas particles:

$$
0.050\,\text{mol}
\rightarrow
0.065\,\text{mol}.
$$

Since the ideal gas law depends on the total number of gas particles,

$$
pV=nRT,
$$

this increase in the number of particles increases the pressure.

This problem showed me that when molecules dissociate, the total mass remains unchanged, but the **number of gas particles changes**.
