# Irodov 1.315 — Flow Through a Bent Tube

[← Back to Hydrodynamics](/irodov/part-1/hydrodynamics.html)

## Problem

An ideal fluid flows along a flat tube of constant cross-section, located in a horizontal plane and bent as shown in Fig. 1.80 (top view).

The flow is steady.

Are the pressures and velocities of the fluid equal at points 1 and 2?

What is the shape of the streamlines?

---

## Physical Idea

At first, it may seem that because the tube has a constant cross-section, the velocities at points 1 and 2 must be equal.

However, continuity only fixes the total volume flow rate, or the average velocity across a cross-section. It does not require the local velocity to be the same everywhere across a curved section of the tube.

The important point is that a fluid particle moving through the bend follows a curved path.

Therefore, it has centripetal acceleration directed toward the center of curvature:

$$
a_c=\frac{v^2}{R}.
$$

This acceleration requires a net inward force.

In an ideal fluid, this inward force is produced by a pressure difference across the bend.

---

## My Solution

### 1. Compare the pressures

Consider a small fluid element moving around the bend.

The pressure on the outer side must be greater than the pressure on the inner side so that the resulting pressure force points toward the center of curvature.

Therefore,

$$
p_{\text{outer}}>p_{\text{inner}}.
$$

Point 1 is closer to the outer side of the bend, while point 2 is closer to the inner side.

Hence,

$$
\boxed{p_1>p_2}.
$$

The pressure difference provides the net inward force required for the centripetal acceleration of the fluid.

---

### 2. Compare the velocities

For the steady ideal flow in the horizontal tube, Bernoulli's equation gives

$$
p+\frac{1}{2}\rho v^2=\text{constant}.
$$

Comparing points 1 and 2,

$$
p_1+\frac{1}{2}\rho v_1^2
=
p_2+\frac{1}{2}\rho v_2^2.
$$

Since

$$
p_1>p_2,
$$

the kinetic-energy term at point 2 must be greater:

$$
\frac{1}{2}\rho v_2^2
>
\frac{1}{2}\rho v_1^2.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{v_2>v_1}.
$$

Thus, the fluid moves faster on the inner side of the bend and slower on the outer side.

---

### 3. Shape of the streamlines

Inside the bend, the fluid particles follow the curvature of the tube.

Therefore, the streamlines in the curved region are approximately concentric circular arcs with different radii.

Before and after the bend, the streamlines become straight.

---

## Final Answer

The pressures at points 1 and 2 are not equal:

$$
\boxed{p_1>p_2}.
$$

The velocities are also not equal:

$$
\boxed{v_2>v_1}.
$$

In the bend, the streamlines are approximately concentric circular arcs.

---

## My Mistake

At first, I thought that because the tube has a constant cross-section, continuity means

$$
v_1=v_2.
$$

However, constant cross-section only fixes the total flow rate and the average velocity across the cross-section. It does not mean that the local velocity must be identical at every point across a bend.

I also initially thought that because point 2 has a smaller radius, it requires a larger centripetal force, so the pressure at point 2 should be larger.

The mistake was treating the pressure itself as the centripetal force.

The centripetal force is actually the **net inward force produced by the pressure difference**. To push the fluid toward the center of curvature, the pressure must be higher on the outer side and lower on the inner side.

Therefore,

$$
p_1>p_2.
$$

---

## What I Learned

- A fluid particle does not follow a curved path by itself. A net inward force is required to change the direction of its velocity.
- In this problem, the pressure difference across the bend provides that inward force.
- Centripetal force is not a separate type of force. It is the name for the net force directed toward the center of curvature.
- Pressure is higher on the outer side of the bend and lower on the inner side.
- The fluid moves faster on the inner side and slower on the outer side.
- Constant tube cross-section does not necessarily mean equal local velocities across a curved section.
- Streamlines represent the paths followed by fluid particles in steady flow, and in the bend they form approximately concentric circular arcs.
