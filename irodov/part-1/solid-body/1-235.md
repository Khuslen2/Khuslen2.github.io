# Irodov 1.235 — Moment and Arm of a Force

[← Back to Dynamics of a Solid Body](/irodov/part-1/solid-body.html)

## Problem

A force

$$
\mathbf{F}=A\mathbf{i}+B\mathbf{j}
$$

is applied to a point whose radius vector relative to the origin O is

$$
\mathbf{r}=a\mathbf{i}+b\mathbf{j},
$$

where a, b, A, and B are constants, and i and j are the unit vectors of the x- and y-axes.

Find the moment N and the arm l of the force relative to the point O.

---

## My Solution

The moment of a force about point O is not found using a dot product.

It is given by the cross product of the radius vector and the force:

$$
\mathbf{N}
=
\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}.
$$

Substituting the given vectors,

$$
\mathbf{r}
=
a\mathbf{i}+b\mathbf{j},
$$

and

$$
\mathbf{F}
=
A\mathbf{i}+B\mathbf{j},
$$

we get

$$
\mathbf{N}
=
(a\mathbf{i}+b\mathbf{j})
\times
(A\mathbf{i}+B\mathbf{j}).
$$

Expanding,

$$
\mathbf{N}
=
aA(\mathbf{i}\times\mathbf{i})
+
aB(\mathbf{i}\times\mathbf{j})
+
bA(\mathbf{j}\times\mathbf{i})
+
bB(\mathbf{j}\times\mathbf{j}).
$$

Since

$$
\mathbf{i}\times\mathbf{i}=0,
$$

$$
\mathbf{j}\times\mathbf{j}=0,
$$

and

$$
\mathbf{i}\times\mathbf{j}=\mathbf{k},
\qquad
\mathbf{j}\times\mathbf{i}=-\mathbf{k},
$$

we obtain

$$
\mathbf{N}
=
aB\mathbf{k}-bA\mathbf{k}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf{N}
=
(aB-bA)\mathbf{k}
}.
$$

The magnitude of the moment is therefore

$$
N
=
|aB-bA|.
$$

---

### Force arm

The arm of a force is the perpendicular distance from point O to the line of action of the force.

The magnitude of the moment is related to the force and its arm by

$$
N=Fl.
$$

The magnitude of the force is

$$
F
=
\sqrt{A^2+B^2}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
l
=
\frac{N}{F}.
$$

Substituting the expression for N,

$$
l
=
\frac{|aB-bA|}
{\sqrt{A^2+B^2}}.
$$

Thus,

$$
\boxed{
l
=
\frac{|aB-bA|}
{\sqrt{A^2+B^2}}
}.
$$

---

## Final Answer

The moment of the force relative to point O is

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf{N}
=
(aB-bA)\mathbf{k}
}.
$$

The arm of the force is

$$
\boxed{
l
=
\frac{|aB-bA|}
{\sqrt{A^2+B^2}}
}.
$$

---

## What I Learned

The important point in this problem is the difference between the **dot product** and the **cross product**.

The dot product

$$
\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{F}
$$

does not give the moment of a force.

The moment is determined by the cross product

$$
\mathbf{N}
=
\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}.
$$

This means that only the part of the force acting perpendicular to the radius vector produces a moment.

I also learned that the force arm is not simply the distance from O to the point where the force is applied.

The arm is the **perpendicular distance from O to the line of action of the force**.

Therefore,

$$
N=Fl
$$

connects the geometrical idea of the force arm with the vector definition of the moment.
