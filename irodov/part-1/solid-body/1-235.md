# Irodov 1.235 — Moment and Arm of a Force

[← Back to Dynamics of a Solid Body](/irodov/part-1/solid-body.html)

## Problem

A force

$$
\mathbf{F}=A\mathbf{i}+B\mathbf{j}
$$

is applied to a point whose radius vector relative to the origin O is

$$
\mathbf{r}=a\mathbf{i}+b\mathbf{j},
$$

where a, b, A, and B are constants, and i and j are the unit vectors of the x- and y-axes.

Find the moment N and the arm l of the force relative to point O.

---

## My Solution

The moment of a force about point O is defined by the cross product of the radius vector and the force:

$$
\mathbf{N}
=
\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}.
$$

Here,

$$
\mathbf{r}
=
a\mathbf{i}+b\mathbf{j}
$$

and

$$
\mathbf{F}
=
A\mathbf{i}+B\mathbf{j}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\mathbf{N}
=
(a\mathbf{i}+b\mathbf{j})
\times
(A\mathbf{i}+B\mathbf{j}).
$$

Expanding,

$$
\mathbf{N}
=
aA(\mathbf{i}\times\mathbf{i})
+
aB(\mathbf{i}\times\mathbf{j})
+
bA(\mathbf{j}\times\mathbf{i})
+
bB(\mathbf{j}\times\mathbf{j}).
$$

We know that

$$
\mathbf{i}\times\mathbf{i}=0,
$$

$$
\mathbf{j}\times\mathbf{j}=0,
$$

$$
\mathbf{i}\times\mathbf{j}=\mathbf{k},
$$

and

$$
\mathbf{j}\times\mathbf{i}=-\mathbf{k}.
$$

Thus,

$$
\mathbf{N}
=
aB\mathbf{k}-bA\mathbf{k}.
$$

Hence,

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf{N}
=
(aB-bA)\mathbf{k}
}.
$$

The magnitude of the moment is therefore

$$
N
=
|aB-bA|.
$$

### Finding the arm of the force

The arm l is the perpendicular distance from point O to the line of action of the force.

The magnitude of the moment can also be written as

$$
N=Fl.
$$

The magnitude of the force is

$$
F
=
\sqrt{A^2+B^2}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
l
=
\frac{N}{F}.
$$

Substituting the magnitude of the moment,

$$
l
=
\frac{|aB-bA|}
{\sqrt{A^2+B^2}}.
$$

Thus,

$$
\boxed{
l
=
\frac{|aB-bA|}
{\sqrt{A^2+B^2}}
}.
$$

---

## Final Answer

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf{N}
=
(aB-bA)\mathbf{k}
}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
l
=
\frac{|aB-bA|}
{\sqrt{A^2+B^2}}
}.
$$

---

## My Mistake

In my handwritten solution, I first wrote

$$
N
=
\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{r}
=
(A\mathbf{i}+B\mathbf{j})
\cdot
(a\mathbf{i}+b\mathbf{j}),
$$

which gives

$$
N=aA+bB.
$$

This was my first mistake.

I used the **dot product**, but the moment of a force is defined using the **cross product**.

The correct expression is

$$
\mathbf{N}
=
\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}.
$$

So instead of

$$
aA+bB,
$$

the moment becomes

$$
\mathbf{N}
=
(aB-bA)\mathbf{k}.
$$

I also wrote

$$
l
=
|\mathbf{r}|
=
\sqrt{a^2+b^2}.
$$

This was my second mistake.

The magnitude of the radius vector gives the distance from O to the point where the force is applied.

However, the **arm of a force is not generally this distance**.

The arm is the perpendicular distance from point O to the line of action of the force.

Therefore, the correct relation is

$$
N=Fl,
$$

so

$$
l
=
\frac{N}{F}
=
\frac{|aB-bA|}
{\sqrt{A^2+B^2}}.
$$

---

## What I Learned

This problem helped me understand that the **moment of a force is fundamentally connected to the cross product**.

The cross product

$$
\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}
$$

measures the turning effect of the force about point O.

If the force acts parallel to the radius vector, then

$$
\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}=0,
$$

so the force produces no moment about O.

If the force acts perpendicular to the radius vector, the moment is maximum.

I also learned the difference between the **radius vector** and the **arm of a force**.

The radius vector connects point O to the point where the force is applied.

The force arm, however, is the perpendicular distance from O to the line along which the force acts.

Therefore, in general,

$$
l\neq|\mathbf{r}|.
$$

Only when the force is perpendicular to the radius vector do we have

$$
l=|\mathbf{r}|.
$$

The relation

$$
N=Fl
$$

is the scalar form of the same idea expressed by

$$
\mathbf{N}
=
\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}.
$$
