## My Mistake

At first, I calculated the moment using the dot product

$$
N=\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{r}.
$$

This was incorrect.

The dot product gives a scalar quantity related to the component of one vector along another. It is not the definition of the moment of a force.

The moment must be calculated using the cross product:

$$
\mathbf{N}
=
\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}.
$$

I also initially assumed that the arm of the force was simply the magnitude of the radius vector,

$$
l=|\mathbf{r}|
=
\sqrt{a^2+b^2}.
$$

This is only true when the force is perpendicular to the radius vector.

In general, the arm is the **perpendicular distance from point O to the line of action of the force**.

Therefore,

$$
l
=
\frac{N}{F}
=
\frac{|aB-bA|}
{\sqrt{A^2+B^2}}.
$$
