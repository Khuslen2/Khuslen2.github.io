# Irodov 1.119 — Work Done on a Locomotive

[← Back to Conservation Laws](/irodov/part-1/conservation.html)

## Problem

A locomotive of mass \(m\) starts moving from rest. Its velocity depends on the distance \(s\) according to

$$
v=a\sqrt{s},
$$

where \(a\) is a constant.

Find the total work performed by all the forces acting on the locomotive during the first \(t\) seconds of motion.

---

## My First Attempt

My first thought was to draw the forces acting on the locomotive and apply Newton's second law.

I introduced a friction force

$$
F_f=kmg,
$$

and wrote

$$
m\ddot{s}=-F_f=-kmg.
$$

From this, I obtained

$$
\ddot{s}=-kg
$$

and then tried to determine the velocity as a function of time.

Finally, I used the work-energy theorem,

$$
W=\Delta K.
$$

However, there is a fundamental problem with this approach:

**the problem never gives a coefficient of friction \(k\), and there is no reason to introduce one.**

I had added a force that was not specified in the problem.

---

## What Went Wrong

The problem asks for the **total work performed by all the forces**.

This is exactly what the work-energy theorem gives:

$$
W_{\mathrm{all}}=\Delta K.
$$

Therefore, I do not need to determine each individual force acting on the locomotive.

Instead, I only need to determine the locomotive's velocity after time \(t\).

The information needed to do that is already given:

$$
v=a\sqrt{s}.
$$

---

## Correct Solution

The velocity is related to displacement by

$$
v=\frac{ds}{dt}.
$$

Using the given relation,

$$
\frac{ds}{dt}=a\sqrt{s}.
$$

Separate the variables:

$$
\frac{ds}{\sqrt{s}}=a\,dt.
$$

Since the locomotive starts from

$$
s=0
$$

at

$$
t=0,
$$

integrate both sides:

$$
\int_0^s\frac{ds'}{\sqrt{s'}}
=
a\int_0^t dt'.
$$

This gives

$$
2\sqrt{s}=at.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\sqrt{s}=\frac{at}{2}.
$$

Now return to the given velocity relation:

$$
v=a\sqrt{s}.
$$

Substituting the expression for \(\sqrt{s}\),

$$
v
=
a\left(\frac{at}{2}\right).
$$

Hence,

$$
\boxed{v(t)=\frac{a^2t}{2}}.
$$

---

## Work-Energy Theorem

The total work performed by all forces is equal to the change in kinetic energy:

$$
W_{\mathrm{all}}=\Delta K.
$$

The locomotive starts from rest, so

$$
K_0=0.
$$

Therefore,

$$
W_{\mathrm{all}}
=
\frac12mv^2.
$$

Substituting

$$
v=\frac{a^2t}{2},
$$

we obtain

$$
W_{\mathrm{all}}
=
\frac12m
\left(\frac{a^2t}{2}\right)^2.
$$

Thus,

$$
W_{\mathrm{all}}
=
\frac12m\frac{a^4t^2}{4}.
$$

Finally,

$$
\boxed{W_{\mathrm{all}}=\frac{ma^4t^2}{8}}.
$$

---

## A Useful Extra Observation

From

$$
v(t)=\frac{a^2t}{2},
$$

the acceleration is

$$
\frac{dv}{dt}
=
\frac{a^2}{2}.
$$

So although the original velocity law

$$
v=a\sqrt{s}
$$

may look unusual, it actually describes motion with **constant acceleration**.

This also gives another way to understand the result.

---

## Dimensional Check

From

$$
v=a\sqrt{s},
$$

the dimensions of \(a\) are

$$
[a]
=
\frac{L/T}{\sqrt{L}}
=
\frac{\sqrt{L}}{T}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
[a^4t^2]
=
\frac{L^2}{T^4}T^2
=
\frac{L^2}{T^2}.
$$

Multiplying by mass,

$$
[ma^4t^2]
=
\frac{ML^2}{T^2},
$$

which has the dimensions of energy.

So the result is dimensionally consistent.

---

## Final Answer

$$
\boxed{W=\frac{ma^4t^2}{8}}
$$

---

## What I Learned

When a problem asks for the **total work done by all forces**, I should immediately consider the work-energy theorem:

$$
W_{\mathrm{net}}=\Delta K.
$$

I do not need to know every individual force if I can determine the change in kinetic energy.

I also learned an important problem-solving rule from my mistake:

> **Do not introduce a physical quantity or force unless the problem or the physics gives a reason for it.**

In this problem, I introduced friction even though no friction coefficient was given. The velocity-distance relation already contained all the information needed to solve the problem.
