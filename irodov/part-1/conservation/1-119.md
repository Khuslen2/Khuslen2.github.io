# Irodov 1.119 — Work and Velocity

[← Back to Conservation Laws](/irodov/part-1/conservation.html)

## Problem

A locomotive of mass \(m\) starts moving from rest. Its velocity depends on the distance \(s\) according to

$$
v=a\sqrt{s},
$$

where \(a\) is a constant.

Find the total work performed by all the forces acting on the locomotive during the first \(t\) seconds of motion.

---

## My First Attempt

My first idea was to draw the forces acting on the locomotive and apply Newton's second law.

I assumed that a friction force opposed the motion and wrote

$$
F_f=kN=kmg,
$$

and then

$$
m\ddot{s}=-F_f=-kmg.
$$

This would give

$$
\ddot{s}=-kg.
$$

Since the locomotive starts from rest, I then tried to obtain its velocity from this acceleration and use the work-energy theorem.

This led me to an expression involving \(g\) and a friction coefficient \(k\).

However, neither \(g\) nor \(k\) is given in the problem.

That was a sign that I had introduced physics that was not actually part of the given information.

---

## What Went Wrong

The problem asks for the **total work performed by all the forces**.

This means that I do not need to determine the individual forces.

The work-energy theorem gives

$$
W_{\mathrm{all}}=\Delta K.
$$

Therefore, all I really need is the velocity of the locomotive after time \(t\).

The problem already gives enough information to determine this through

$$
v=a\sqrt{s}.
$$

So introducing an unknown friction force was unnecessary.

---

## Correct Solution

The velocity is given as a function of distance:

$$
v=a\sqrt{s}.
$$

But velocity is also

$$
v=\frac{ds}{dt}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\frac{ds}{dt}=a\sqrt{s}.
$$

Separate the variables:

$$
\frac{ds}{\sqrt{s}}=a\,dt.
$$

The locomotive starts at

$$
s=0
$$

when

$$
t=0.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\int_0^s\frac{ds'}{\sqrt{s'}}
=
a\int_0^t dt'.
$$

Integrating,

$$
2\sqrt{s}=at.
$$

Hence,

$$
\sqrt{s}=\frac{at}{2}.
$$

Now return to the original relation

$$
v=a\sqrt{s}.
$$

Substituting \(\sqrt{s}=at/2\),

$$
v
=
a\left(\frac{at}{2}\right).
$$

Thus,

$$
\boxed{v(t)=\frac{a^2t}{2}}.
$$

---

## Work-Energy Theorem

The total work performed by all forces equals the change in kinetic energy:

$$
W_{\mathrm{all}}=\Delta K.
$$

Since the locomotive starts from rest,

$$
K_0=0.
$$

Therefore,

$$
W_{\mathrm{all}}
=
\frac12 mv^2.
$$

Using

$$
v=\frac{a^2t}{2},
$$

we obtain

$$
W_{\mathrm{all}}
=
\frac12m
\left(
\frac{a^2t}{2}
\right)^2.
$$

Therefore,

$$
W_{\mathrm{all}}
=
\frac12m
\frac{a^4t^2}{4}.
$$

Finally,

$$
\boxed{
W_{\mathrm{all}}
=
\frac{ma^4t^2}{8}
}
$$

---

## Dimensional Check

The constant \(a\) in

$$
v=a\sqrt{s}
$$

does **not** have the dimensions of acceleration.

Since

$$
[a]
=
\frac{[v]}{\sqrt{[s]}},
$$

we have

$$
[a]
=
\frac{L/T}{\sqrt{L}}
=
\frac{\sqrt{L}}{T}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
[a^4]
=
\frac{L^2}{T^4}.
$$

The dimensions of the final expression are

$$
[ma^4t^2]
=
M
\frac{L^2}{T^4}
T^2
=
\frac{ML^2}{T^2},
$$

which are the dimensions of energy.

So the result is dimensionally consistent.

---

## Final Answer

$$
\boxed{
W=\frac{ma^4t^2}{8}
}
$$

---

## What I Learned

My main mistake was introducing a force that the problem never specified.

Seeing a moving object does not mean that I should automatically invent friction or try to identify every force acting on it.

The phrase **"total work performed by all forces"** is an important clue because the work-energy theorem directly relates the total work to the change in kinetic energy:

$$
W_{\mathrm{all}}=\Delta K.
$$

I also learned to use the given relation before introducing additional assumptions.

Here, the equation

$$
v=a\sqrt{s}
$$

already contains all the information needed to determine \(v(t)\).

A useful rule for future problems:

> **Use the information actually given before introducing a new physical assumption.**
