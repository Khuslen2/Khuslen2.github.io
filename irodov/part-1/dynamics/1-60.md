# Irodov 1.60 — Three Connected Bodies

[← Back to Dynamics](/irodov/part-1/dynamics.html)

## Problem

Three bodies of equal mass are connected by light threads as shown in the arrangement.

The pulley and threads are ideal, and the pulley is frictionless.

The coefficient of friction between the two bodies on the horizontal surface and the surface is $k$.

Find:

1. the acceleration $w$ with which the hanging body $m_0$ moves downward,
2. the tension $T_2$ in the thread connecting $m_1$ and $m_2$.

Consider the possible cases.

---

## Setup

Let

$$
m_0=m_1=m_2=m.
$$

Assume first that $m_0$ moves downward.

Then $m_1$ and $m_2$ move toward the pulley with the same acceleration magnitude $w$.

Let

- $T_1$ be the tension in the thread connecting $m_0$ and $m_1$,
- $T_2$ be the tension in the thread connecting $m_1$ and $m_2$.

The friction forces acting on $m_1$ and $m_2$ oppose their motion.

Since

$$
N_1=mg,
$$

and

$$
N_2=mg,
$$

the friction forces are

$$
F_{f1}=kmg
$$

and

$$
F_{f2}=kmg.
$$

---

## My Approach

I applied Newton's second law separately to all three bodies.

### Body $m_2$

For $m_2$, taking the direction toward the pulley as positive,

$$
mw=T_2-F_{f2}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
mw=T_2-kmg.
$$

---

### Body $m_1$

For $m_1$,

$$
mw=T_1-T_2-F_{f1}.
$$

Thus,

$$
mw=T_1-T_2-kmg.
$$

---

### Hanging body $m_0$

For $m_0$, taking downward as positive,

$$
mw=mg-T_1.
$$

---

## Finding the Acceleration

Adding the three equations,

$$
mw+mw+mw
=
(T_2-kmg)
+
(T_1-T_2-kmg)
+
(mg-T_1).
$$

The internal tensions $T_1$ and $T_2$ cancel.

Therefore,

$$
3mw=mg-2kmg.
$$

Dividing by $m$,

$$
3w=g(1-2k).
$$

Hence,

$$
\boxed{
w=\frac{g}{3}(1-2k)
}
$$

---

## Possible Cases

The result depends on the value of $k$.

### Case 1: $k<\frac12$

Then

$$
1-2k>0,
$$

so

$$
w>0.
$$

Therefore, the assumed direction is correct: $m_0$ moves downward.

The acceleration is

$$
\boxed{
w=\frac{g}{3}(1-2k)
}.
$$

### Case 2: $k=\frac12$

Then

$$
w=0.
$$

This is the limiting case between downward motion and equilibrium.

### Case 3: $k>\frac12$

The expression would give

$$
w<0.
$$

This means that the initial assumption that $m_0$ moves downward is not valid.

If the system is initially at rest and the available static friction is sufficient, the system does not begin moving downward.

---

## Tension Between $m_1$ and $m_2$

From the equation for $m_2$,

$$
mw=T_2-kmg.
$$

Therefore,

$$
T_2=mw+kmg.
$$

Substituting

$$
w=\frac{g}{3}(1-2k),
$$

we obtain

$$
T_2
=
m\frac{g}{3}(1-2k)+kmg.
$$

Factor out $mg$:

$$
T_2
=
mg
\left[
\frac{1-2k}{3}+k
\right].
$$

Therefore,

$$
T_2
=
mg
\frac{1+k}{3}.
$$

Hence,

$$
\boxed{
T_2=\frac{mg}{3}(1+k)
}
$$

for the case $k<\frac12$ when the system moves with $m_0$ downward.

---

## Final Answer

For

$$
k<\frac12,
$$

the hanging body moves downward with acceleration

$$
\boxed{
w=\frac{g}{3}(1-2k)
}
$$

and the tension in the thread connecting $m_1$ and $m_2$ is

$$
\boxed{
T_2=\frac{mg}{3}(1+k)
}.
$$

The limiting case occurs at

$$
\boxed{k=\frac12}.
$$

---

## What I Learned

The easiest way to determine the acceleration was to write Newton's second law for each body separately and then add the equations.

When the equations are added, the internal tensions cancel automatically:

$$
+T_1-T_1=0,
$$

$$
+T_2-T_2=0.
$$

This lets the three-body system be treated as one whole system when finding the acceleration.

The individual equations are still useful afterward because one of them can be used to calculate the tension.

Another important point is to check whether the assumed direction of motion is consistent with the final expression.

Here,

$$
w=\frac{g}{3}(1-2k)
$$

immediately shows that the assumed downward motion of $m_0$ is possible only when

$$
k<\frac12.
$$
