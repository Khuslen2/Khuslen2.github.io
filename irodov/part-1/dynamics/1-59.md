# Irodov 1.59 — Aerostat and Ballast

[← Back to Irodov problems](/)

## Problem

An aerostat of mass $m$ is moving downward with constant acceleration of magnitude $w$.

A certain ballast mass is then dumped so that the aerostat begins accelerating upward with the same magnitude $w$.

Neglect air resistance.

Find the ballast mass that must be dumped.

---

## My First Attempt

Let $m'$ be the ballast mass to be dumped.

I chose the vertical axis upward.

At first, since the aerostat accelerates downward with magnitude $w$, I wrote

$$
F_y = -mw.
$$

After dumping the ballast, I wrote

$$
F_y = (m-m')w.
$$

Then I equated the two expressions and obtained

$$
-m = m-m'.
$$

Therefore,

$$
m' = 2m.
$$

At this point, I felt that the result was suspicious.

A ballast mass larger than the entire original mass of the aerostat is physically impossible, so something in my force model had to be wrong.

---

## What Went Wrong

The mistake was that I treated the **net force** as though it were an individual physical force that stayed the same before and after the ballast was dumped.

But the aerostat is actually acted on by two main forces:

- the buoyant force $F_B$ upward,
- the gravitational force $mg$ downward.

The buoyant force stays essentially the same when the ballast is dumped, while the weight changes because the mass changes.

So I need to write Newton's second law using the actual forces.

---

## Correct Solution

Take the upward direction as positive.

Before dumping the ballast, the aerostat has mass $m$ and acceleration

$$
a_y = -w.
$$

Newton's second law gives

$$
F_B - mg = -mw.
$$

Therefore,

$$
F_B = m(g-w).
$$

After dumping ballast mass $m'$, the new mass becomes

$$
m-m'.
$$

Now the aerostat accelerates upward with magnitude $w$, so

$$
a_y = +w.
$$

Newton's second law gives

$$
F_B - (m-m')g = (m-m')w.
$$

Substituting

$$
F_B = m(g-w),
$$

we obtain

$$
m(g-w) - (m-m')g = (m-m')w.
$$

Expanding,

$$
mg - mw - mg + m'g = mw - m'w.
$$

Therefore,

$$
m'g - mw = mw - m'w.
$$

Collecting the terms containing $m'$,

$$
m'g + m'w = 2mw.
$$

Factoring,

$$
m'(g+w) = 2mw.
$$

Hence,

$$
m' = \frac{2mw}{g+w}.
$$

---

## Final Answer

$$
\boxed{
m' = \frac{2mw}{g+w}
}
$$

---

## What I Learned

This problem reminded me that Newton's second law must be applied to the **actual forces** acting on the body.

The net force is not a separate force by itself.

My original mistake was forgetting to include gravity explicitly and then treating the net force as though it remained unchanged after the ballast was dumped.

The impossible result

$$
m' = 2m
$$

was an important warning sign.

A quick physical check of the final answer can sometimes reveal a mistake even before redoing the algebra.

For dynamics problems, I want to build the habit of writing the force diagram first:

$$
\uparrow F_B
$$

$$
\downarrow mg
$$

and only then applying

$$
F_{\mathrm{net}} = ma.
$$
