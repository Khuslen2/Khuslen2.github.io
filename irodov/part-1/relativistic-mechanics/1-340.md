# Irodov Problem 1.340 — Length Contraction

## Problem

A rod moves along its own length with constant velocity \(u\) relative to an inertial reference frame. Find the velocity at which its measured length in that frame is \(0.5\%\) shorter than its proper length.

---

## Physical Idea

The **proper length** \(L_0\) is the length of the rod measured in the reference frame where the rod is at rest.

When the rod moves parallel to its length with velocity \(u\), an observer in another inertial frame measures a contracted length:

$$
L = L_0\sqrt{1-\frac{u^2}{c^2}}.
$$

Since the rod is \(0.5\%\) shorter than its proper length,

$$
L = (1-0.005)L_0 = 0.995L_0.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\frac{L}{L_0}=0.995.
$$

---

## My Solution

Using the relativistic length-contraction formula,

$$
L=L_0\sqrt{1-\frac{u^2}{c^2}}.
$$

Dividing both sides by \(L_0\),

$$
\frac{L}{L_0}
=
\sqrt{1-\frac{u^2}{c^2}}.
$$

Since

$$
\frac{L}{L_0}=0.995,
$$

we have

$$
\sqrt{1-\frac{u^2}{c^2}}=0.995.
$$

Squaring both sides,

$$
1-\frac{u^2}{c^2}=(0.995)^2.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\frac{u^2}{c^2}
=
1-(0.995)^2,
$$

and hence

$$
u=c\sqrt{1-(0.995)^2}.
$$

Using

$$
c=3.00\times10^8\ \text{m/s},
$$

gives

$$
u
=
3.00\times10^8
\sqrt{1-(0.995)^2}.
$$

Thus,

$$
u\approx2.996\times10^7\ \text{m/s}.
$$

Equivalently,

$$
u\approx0.0999c.
$$

---

## Final Answer

$$
\boxed{u\approx3.0\times10^7\ \text{m/s}\approx0.100c}
$$

---

## What I Learned

Even a seemingly small relativistic length contraction requires a surprisingly large velocity. A contraction of only \(0.5\%\) already requires the rod to move at about \(10\%\) of the speed of light.

I also learned that \(L_0\) should be called the **proper length**, rather than simply the initial length. The proper length is specifically the length measured in the object's own rest frame.
