# Irodov 1.200 — Circular Orbit Around the Sun

[← Back to Universal Gravitation](/irodov/part-1/gravitation.html)

## Problem

A planet moves in a circular orbit around the Sun with speed

$$
v=34.9\,\mathrm{km/s}.
$$

Find the period of revolution of the planet around the Sun.

---

## My Approach

For a circular orbit, the gravitational force of the Sun provides the centripetal force.

Let

- $M$ = mass of the planet,
- $M_s$ = mass of the Sun,
- $R$ = orbital radius,
- $v$ = orbital speed.

The gravitational force is

$$
F_g
=
G\frac{MM_s}{R^2}.
$$

The required centripetal force is

$$
F_c
=
\frac{Mv^2}{R}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
G\frac{MM_s}{R^2}
=
\frac{Mv^2}{R}.
$$

Canceling $M$ and one factor of $R$,

$$
\frac{GM_s}{R}
=
v^2.
$$

Hence,

$$
R
=
\frac{GM_s}{v^2}.
$$

---

## Finding the Period

The period of uniform circular motion is

$$
T
=
\frac{2\pi R}{v}.
$$

Substituting

$$
R=\frac{GM_s}{v^2},
$$

gives

$$
T
=
\frac{2\pi GM_s}{v^3}.
$$

---

## My Numerical Mistake

In my first calculation, I substituted

$$
v=34.9
$$

directly into the equation while using SI values for $G$ and $M_s$.

That gave

$$
T\approx1.96\times10^{16}\,\mathrm{s},
$$

which is incorrect.

The problem gives the velocity in kilometres per second, but the other quantities are in SI units.

Therefore,

$$
34.9\,\mathrm{km/s}
=
34.9\times10^3\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

Because the velocity appears as $v^3$, missing the factor of $10^3$ changes the result by

$$
(10^3)^3=10^9.
$$

This is why my original answer was too large by a factor of one billion.

---

## Correct Calculation

Using

$$
G=6.6743\times10^{-11}\,
\mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}},
$$

$$
M_s=1.989\times10^{30}\,\mathrm{kg},
$$

and

$$
v=34.9\times10^3\,\mathrm{m/s},
$$

we obtain

$$
T
=
\frac{
2\pi
(6.6743\times10^{-11})
(1.989\times10^{30})
}{
(34.9\times10^3)^3
}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
T
\approx
1.96\times10^7\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

Converting to days,

$$
T
=
\frac{1.96\times10^7}
{86400}
\approx
227\,\mathrm{days}.
$$

---

## Final Answer

$$
\boxed{
T\approx1.96\times10^7\,\mathrm{s}
}
$$

or approximately

$$
\boxed{
T\approx227\,\mathrm{days}
}.
$$

---

## What I Learned

The physics of my original solution was correct:

$$
F_g=F_c.
$$

The mistake happened only when I substituted the numerical values.

When a formula is evaluated using SI constants such as $G$, every quantity must first be converted to SI units.

This problem was also a good reminder that errors in powers can become very large.

Since

$$
T\propto\frac{1}{v^3},
$$

a unit error in velocity is cubed in the final answer.

So before substituting numbers, I should always check the units first.
